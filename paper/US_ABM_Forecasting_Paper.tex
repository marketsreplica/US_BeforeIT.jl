\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We build an agent-based macroeconomic model of the United States on the framework of
\citet{poledna2023} and evaluate it under that paper's own out-of-sample protocol: 61 quarterly
forecast origins from 2010Q2 to 2025Q2, horizons of 1 to 12 quarters, 500 free-running stochastic
paths per origin, and a twenty-column comparator field scored on identical cells. The model is
calibrated from public data under a strict no-fitting rule --- no behavioural parameter ever sees
a forecast error --- and completes the reference framework with three structural components that a
United States implementation turns out to require: an opening supply--use system reconciled by
biproportional (RAS) adjustment, because the published BEA tables do not balance once bridged
across valuation bases and projected onto commodities; growth expectations formed by a random walk
with drift, because the reference log-level autoregression is biased low by construction on
trending data; and observed trend growth in supply --- labour productivity, wages, and capacity
efficiency advancing at an origin-local estimate of measured productivity-plus-labour-force
growth --- without which a fixed-population model must either exhaust its labour pool or drain its
capacity headroom. The comparator field contains ten statistical benchmarks and two dynamic
stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) models re-estimated recursively at every origin, including a
full \citet{smetswouters2007} system granted more data than any other column. On the headline pair
\{real GDP growth, GDP-deflator inflation\} the ABM attains the best weighted RMSE ratio of all
twenty columns in all three sample tracks (0.828, 0.828, 0.792 against a VAR(1) anchor);
Romano--Wolf multiplicity-controlled inference separates it from both DSGE columns at the 5\,\%
level in every track, while its margin over the best statistical columns remains marginal
(adjusted $p \approx 0.08$). Its predictive densities are the sharpest in the field by continuous
ranked probability score, but its GDP-deflator intervals are under-dispersed (90\,\% coverage
0.70--0.79), so a preregistered superiority bar fails on that single interval-calibration leg and
the claim retained is the conditional one. The headline conclusion is unchanged under 2017 and
2012 input--output structures (weighted ratios move by at most 0.0007), and matched-seed component
experiments attribute it: the accounting reconciliation carries most of the accuracy, the
expectation specification most of the rest, and the supply-growth component --- which repairs
unemployment dynamics and long-run capacity validity --- is deliberately headline-neutral. All of
this is a revised-data, mixed-vintage diagnostic, not a real-time forecasting claim.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} agent-based macroeconomics; out-of-sample forecast evaluation;
DSGE benchmarking; input--output reconciliation; balanced growth; forecast-comparison inference.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The Poledna programme}

Macroeconomic forecasting is dominated by two model classes. \emph{Time-series} models --- vector
autoregressions (VARs) and their Bayesian variants --- treat the economy as a low-dimensional
stochastic process and extrapolate its estimated dynamics; they are accurate at short horizons
and structurally mute. \emph{Dynamic stochastic general equilibrium} (DSGE) models
\citep{smetswouters2007} write down a small number of optimising representative agents, impose
market clearing at every date, and estimate the resulting cross-equation restrictions; they are
structurally interpretable and, at forecast horizons, rarely better than a well-specified VAR.

\emph{Agent-based models} (ABMs) take a third route \citep{farmerfoley2009}. They simulate a
population of heterogeneous entities --- firms, households, a bank, a government --- each following
explicit behavioural rules, and let aggregates emerge from decentralised interaction. Markets do
not clear by assumption; they clear, or fail to clear, through search and matching, with rationing
as an observable outcome. For two decades this class was excluded from the forecasting
conversation on the grounds that it was unfalsifiable in the only way that matters: nobody had run
one against the standard benchmarks on a rolling out-of-sample grid.

\citet{poledna2023} closed that gap. They calibrated a large ABM of Austria one-to-one against the
national accounts, ran free-running forecasts from quarterly origins over 2010Q2--2016Q4 at
horizons $h \in \{1,2,4,8,12\}$ with 500 stochastic paths each, and scored the ensemble mean
against AR, VAR and Bayesian DSGE benchmarks. The ABM was competitive, and better than the DSGE at
most horizons. That result is the reference point of the present work, and its protocol is the one
reproduced here.

\subsection{What this paper does}

This paper ports that programme to the United States and answers its question at full strength:
\emph{does the ABM forecast the U.S.\ economy better than statistical and equilibrium challengers,
on identical cells, under a protocol frozen in advance?} Five things are new relative to a naive
port, and they organise the paper.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The model, completed for the United States} (\S\ref{sec:model}). The BeforeIT
      implementation \citep{beforeit2025} of the Poledna framework is retained whole; three
      structural components are added, each forced by a property of the U.S.\ setting and each
      calibrated from observations or accounting identities alone: a reconciled opening
      supply--use system, drift-form growth expectations, and observed trend growth in supply ---
      productivity, wages and capacity efficiency advancing at a rate estimated from measured
      productivity and labour-force growth, the latter carrying the demographics-and-immigration
      margin.
\item \textbf{Calibration under a no-fitting firewall} (\S\ref{sec:calib}--\S\ref{sec:austria}).
      Every parameter is read from public data, computed from an identity, or estimated from
      pre-origin history by a fixed rule; no behavioural parameter ever sees a forecast error, and
      the claim is verified numerically rather than asserted.
\item \textbf{A twenty-column comparator field} (\S\ref{sec:design}). Ten statistical benchmarks;
      two DSGE models re-estimated by posterior mode at every origin --- an
      \citet{anschorfheide2007}-type small New Keynesian model and a full 49-state
      \citet{smetswouters2007} system that is deliberately granted more history and more series
      than any other column; and the ABM's mean and median ensemble columns. All columns are
      scored on identical common cells over 61 origins and three sample tracks.
\item \textbf{Results with formal inference and densities} (\S\ref{sec:results}). Point standings
      with HLN-corrected Diebold--Mariano tests, Romano--Wolf multiplicity control and a model
      confidence set; exact ensemble CRPS and interval coverage from the retained predictive
      draws; unemployment scored and reported honestly; and a structural-vintage robustness check
      that re-runs the whole exercise under 2017 and 2012 input--output structures.
\item \textbf{Component attribution} (\S\ref{sec:evidence}). Matched-seed, identical-cell
      experiments that switch each component off --- and two demand-side investment designs that
      were built and rejected on stability grounds --- so that the headline accuracy is
      attributed to mechanisms rather than asserted of the model as a whole.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the reader should take away, and what they should not}

The ABM ranks first of twenty on the headline target pair in all three sample tracks, its margin
over both DSGE columns is significant at the 5\,\% level under multiplicity control, and its
predictive densities are the sharpest in the field by CRPS. That is a real result on a matched
grid, and it is also a \emph{revised-data, mixed-vintage diagnostic}: the data are current-vintage
throughout, the annual structure is carried backwards to historical origins (a dependence measured
directly in \S\ref{sec:vintage} and found immaterial for the input--output block), and no
real-time information set was reconstructed. The margin over the best \emph{statistical} columns
is marginal under multiplicity control (adjusted $p \approx 0.08$); the GDP-deflator's predictive
intervals are under-dispersed, which fails one leg of the preregistered superiority bar; and on
the unemployment \emph{level} the ABM loses to simple persistence models, for a measured
initialisation reason. These limits are stated in \S\ref{sec:limits} and are not incidental to the
result; they are part of it.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

This section is a compact overview sufficient to read the results. The full equation system is in
\citet{poledna2023} and its implementation in \citet{beforeit2025}; the behavioural block is
inherited whole, plus two flag-gated specification choices (the growth expectation of
\S\ref{sec:expectations} and the trend-growth component of \S\ref{sec:trendgrowth}) that are off
by default, so the Austrian and Italian calibrations of the library remain bit-identical.

\subsection{Agents and the flow of a quarter}

The economy contains six classes of agent.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Firms.] $\sum_s I_s$ firms distributed over $S$ industries. Each firm $i$ in industry $s$
  produces a single commodity with a \emph{Leontief} technology \citep{leontief1936}: output
  requires labour, intermediate inputs and capital services in fixed proportions, with no
  substitution between them. Concretely, output is capped by the binding factor,
  \begin{equation}
    Y_i = \min\!\Big( \underbrace{\alpha_s N_i}_{\text{labour}},\;
                      \underbrace{\beta_s M_i}_{\text{materials}},\;
                      \underbrace{\kappa_s K_i}_{\text{capital}} \Big),
    \label{eq:leontief}
  \end{equation}
  where $N_i$ is employment, $M_i$ the intermediate-input bundle, $K_i$ the capital stock, and
  $(\alpha_s,\beta_s,\kappa_s)$ are industry productivities read off the input--output table. The
  third term is the \emph{capacity envelope}; how it is initialised, and how it grows, are two of
  the design decisions this paper measures (\S\ref{sec:calib-balance}, \S\ref{sec:trendgrowth}).
\item[Households.] Worker households supply labour and consume; inactive households receive
  transfers and consume; firm owners receive dividends. Consumption follows a propensity rule on
  disposable income and liquid wealth, allocated across commodities by a fixed basket $b^{HH}_g$.
\item[A bank.] One consolidated commercial bank intermediates deposits and loans subject to a
  capital-adequacy constraint, and rations credit when the constraint binds.
\item[A central bank.] Sets the policy rate by a Taylor rule --- a feedback rule raising the rate
  when inflation or an activity measure exceeds target --- with inertia $\rho$, an intercept
  $r^{*}$, and response coefficients $\xi_\pi$ and $\xi_\gamma$.
\item[A government.] Collects taxes, pays wages, transfers and unemployment benefits, consumes a
  commodity basket $c^{G}_g$, and issues debt.
\item[A rest-of-world block.] Exports absorb a basket $c^{E}_g$; imports supply $c^{I}_g$. In this
  calibration the block is exogenous and is driven by estimated univariate processes.
\end{description}

Within a quarter, firms form expectations, choose target output and desired inputs, post vacancies
and demand credit; the labour, credit and goods markets then clear through decentralised
\emph{search and matching} --- buyers meet a random subset of sellers, transact at posted prices,
and any unmet demand is simply not satisfied. Rationing is therefore a first-class observable of
the model, reported here as \emph{fill rates}. Insolvent firms exit and are replaced.

Two structural facts about this core shape everything downstream, and both are measured rather
than assumed in \S\ref{sec:evidence}. First, the capacity envelope is anchored to the
\emph{opening output composition}: if the opening demand system asks sectors for quantities
inconsistent with that composition, the one-sided $\min$ prevents idle capacity in one sector from
compensating a binding ceiling in another, and nothing in the behavioural block can reconcile the
two. The opening accounts must therefore balance --- an accounting precondition the calibration
of \S\ref{sec:calib-balance} enforces exactly. Second, with replacement-only investment the
envelope never moves on its own: over every experimental variant run for this work,
$K_{\text{end}}/K_1 = 1.0000$--$1.0009$. Whatever trend growth the economy is to have must
therefore enter through the productivity coefficients, which is what \S\ref{sec:trendgrowth}
does --- and \S\ref{sec:evidence-invest} reports why the alternative, letting investment itself
respond to demand, was built and rejected.

\subsection{Growth expectations: a random walk with drift}
\label{sec:expectations}

Firms extrapolate aggregate demand: target output is desired sales scaled by an economy-wide
expected growth rate $\gamma_e$, re-estimated every quarter from the model's own realised output
history. The specification matters more than it looks. The reference implementation estimates an
ordinary-least-squares AR(1) with constant on the \emph{log level} of output --- a series that is,
in statistical terms, integrated with drift (its level wanders; its growth rate is roughly
stationary around a positive mean). For a log-linear trend $x_t = a + g t$, that estimator has the
closed-form expectation
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_e \;\approx\; g\Big[\,1-(1-\hat\alpha)\tfrac{T}{2}\,\Big],
  \label{eq:gammae}
\end{equation}
where $\hat\alpha$ is the fitted autoregressive coefficient and $T$ the estimation window. Because
$\hat\alpha$ sits below one in any finite sample --- the \citet{kendall1954} small-sample bias,
compounded by the level shocks of 2008--09 and 2020 --- the bracket is strictly less than one:
\emph{the estimator delivers only a fraction of the trend it was shown}, and the fraction
\emph{falls} as the window lengthens. Measured across six representative origins the delivered
fraction is 32--63\,\% (Table~\ref{tab:gammae}), reproduced by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammae} to
0.05\pp. Shorter windows do not help: they trade a smaller $T$ against a more biased $\hat\alpha$.

The model therefore uses the correctly specified estimator for an integrated-with-drift series:
a \emph{random walk with drift}, $\hat\alpha$ pinned to one and the drift taken as average past
log growth, on past data only. At the same six origins this recovers $+2.33$ to $+2.42\,\%$
annualised --- the trend, at every origin. The choice consumes exactly one Normal variate per
quarter on either branch, a deliberate property that keeps matched-seed component experiments
aligned (\S\ref{sec:evidence}); it is gated behind a model property that is default-off; and its
measured contribution to forecast accuracy is reported in \S\ref{sec:evidence-attrib} rather than
folded silently into the model.

\subsection{Trend growth in supply}
\label{sec:trendgrowth}

The reference model, ported literally, has no trend growth in supply anywhere: labour productivity
$\alpha_s$, the wage base $\bar w_s$, the labour force, and the capacity envelope $K\kappa$ are
all frozen at their opening values for the entire simulation. For Austria over a twelve-quarter
horizon this is a tolerable abstraction. For the United States it is not, and the failure is
arithmetic, not aesthetic. U.S.\ trend output growth of roughly 2\,\% per year has two measured
supply-side sources --- output per worker (productivity) and the number of workers (population and
immigration) --- in roughly comparable parts. A model in which neither source exists can grow only
by working its fixed labour pool and fixed capacity harder: simulated demand growth must be met
one-for-one by employment growth, so unemployment falls mechanically quarter after quarter until
the pool is exhausted, while output growth drains the fixed 17.6\,\% capacity headroom until
utilisation hits its ceiling. Both pathologies are measured directly in \S\ref{sec:evidence-gates};
each invalidates a different part of the model --- the labour market within the forecast window,
the growth mechanism beyond it.

The model therefore registers observed trend growth on the supply side, per origin, from data
available at the origin. Let
\begin{equation}
  g \;=\; \underbrace{\overline{\Delta\ln\!\big(\mathrm{GDP}/\mathrm{employment}\big)}}_{\text{labour productivity}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\overline{\Delta\ln\!\big(\mathrm{labour\ force}\big)}}_{\text{demographics and immigration}},
  \label{eq:trendg}
\end{equation}
each term a trailing 40-quarter mean ending at the forecast origin, computed from measured series
(real GDP, civilian employment, civilian labour force; sources in \S\ref{sec:calib-trend}). At the
start of every simulated quarter the supply coefficients advance by this rate:
$\alpha_s$ and $\bar w_s$ grow by $(1+g)$ --- so output per worker and the wage level track trend
while \emph{unit labour cost} $\bar w/\alpha$, the object that drives pricing, is invariant ---
and $\kappa_s$ and $\delta_s$ grow by the same factor --- so productive capacity $K\kappa$ tracks
trend on constant book capital while the replacement share $\delta/\kappa$ (capital consumption
per unit of output) is exactly stable. No demand flow is injected anywhere, and the accounting
identity $\text{net }I=\Delta K$ holds trivially in book units.

Three properties make this the right closure rather than a convenient one. \emph{It is observed,
not fitted}: $g$ is a trailing average of measured series, re-estimated at each origin by a fixed
rule frozen before any forecast was scored; across the 61 origins it runs 1.5--2.3\,\% annualised,
and its labour-force component --- the immigration margin --- tripled between the 2020 and 2025
origins (labour-force growth averaged 1.26\,\%/yr over 2022--2024 against 0.75\,\%/yr over
2011--2019), which the origin-local window picks up automatically. \emph{It is balanced}: demand
trends in the model (household income, government consumption, exports) already grow at observed
rates through the estimated exogenous processes; giving supply the same measured trend removes a
structural asymmetry rather than adding a stimulus. \emph{It is falsifiable in components}:
switching the mechanism off collapses unemployment and utilisation exactly as the arithmetic
predicts, and the demand-side alternative --- letting desired investment respond to the capacity
gap --- destabilises the economy; both experiments are reported in \S\ref{sec:evidence}.

condition the simulator needs: the technology matrix, final-demand baskets, agent counts, tax and

carries 68 observed commodities and 71 industries; the four retail industry columns

\subsection{The opening accounts must balance, and are made to}
\label{sec:calib-balance}

An input--output system published by a statistical agency balances \emph{in the form in which it
is published}: every commodity's supply equals its uses, in one valuation basis, on one set of
axes. The U.S.\ system is published at purchasers' prices over non-identical industry and
commodity axes, while the model's coefficients are defined at basic prices on a symmetric
commodity basis (\S\ref{sec:austria} details the bridge). After the required valuation bridge and
industry-to-commodity projection --- and after the decision to take commodity imports from the
measured BEA T262 import matrix rather than solving them as a residual, which removes the last
free slack variable --- the balance is no longer guaranteed. In the raw U.S.\ artifact it fails
badly: modelled uses differ from supply by $-70.6\,\%$ to $+47.6\,\%$ across the 68 commodities,
with a cross-sector standard deviation of 17.9 percentage points.

An equilibrium or time-series model would never notice. This model must: the opening demand
composition and the opening capacity envelope are \emph{both} anchored to the same accounts
(\S\ref{sec:model}), so a sector asked for 47\,\% more than its recorded output sits permanently
against its capacity ceiling while a sector asked for less idles, and the one-sided production
constraint lets the shortfalls accumulate without offset. Left in place, this single accounting
defect costs about $-2.75$ percentage points of annualised real-growth forecast bias; the measured
anatomy is in \S\ref{sec:evidence-recon}.

The calibration therefore reconciles the use side exactly, by \emph{biproportional adjustment} ---
the RAS method of \citet{stone1961} and \citet{bacharach1970}, equivalently the
minimum-relative-entropy projection: find the use matrix closest to the observed one, in the
information-theoretic sense, that satisfies the accounting margins. Formally, with
$U^{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^{G\times J}$ the observed use matrix over $G=68$ commodities and
$J=72$ users (68 industry intermediate columns plus consumption $C$, government $G$, investment
$I$ and exports $X$),
\begin{equation}
  U^{\star} \;=\; \arg\min_{U\ge 0}\;\sum_{g,j} U_{gj}\log\frac{U_{gj}}{U^{0}_{gj}}
  \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
  \sum_j U_{gj} = r_g \;\;\forall g,
  \qquad \sum_g U_{gj} = c_j \;\;\forall j,
  \label{eq:ras}
\end{equation}
with row controls $r_g = \text{industry output}_g + \text{measured imports}_g$ and column controls
$c_j$ the measured budgets. The solution has the biproportional form
$U^{\star}_{gj}=\rho_g\,U^{0}_{gj}\,s_j$, is obtained by alternately scaling rows and columns, and
preserves structural zeros exactly. Crucially, the measured import levels are \emph{retained}: the
reconciliation works around the measured trade data rather than discarding it.

\centering
\caption{The RAS reconciliation as executed, from the committed reconciliation report. The

One accounting choice is required before RAS can run at all: row and column controls must sum to
the same total, and in the raw artifact they do not --- uses exceed supply by 1.0104\,\% in
aggregate. That excess decomposes \emph{exactly} (to $5.6\times10^{-9}$) into two measurement
facts of the source data: the accounts' own production-versus-expenditure statistical discrepancy
(\$442.9bn, 1.57\,\% of GDP) and negative net non-dwelling fixed investment (capital consumption
exceeding non-dwelling gross fixed capital formation by \$88.2bn). One control must move, and the
calibration anchors on the production account, scaling the four final-demand aggregates (and the
two components that set the investment budget) by
\begin{equation}
  \lambda \;=\; \frac{\text{supply} - \text{intermediates}}{C+G+I+X} \;=\; 0.983460 .
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
$\lambda$ is fixed by the accounting identity alone; its semantics are recorded verbatim in every
result manifest. The alternative closure --- hold every measured budget and let a uniform
1.01\,\% excess-demand wedge survive --- was built and measured too; it overshoots, because a
uniform 1\,\% excess demand is itself a growth impulse (\S\ref{sec:evidence-gates}).

largest single commodity adjustments are professional and technical services (uses cut 32.3\,\%,
closing a $+47.6\,\%$ gap), wholesale trade (raised 42.5\,\%, closing a $-29.8\,\%$ gap) and state
and local government (raised 26.2\,\%).

\subsection{United States--specific choices in the artifact}

Three further choices depart from the reference specification.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
\item[Explicit measured trade (\code{use_explicit_trade = true}).] Commodity imports are taken
  from the measured BEA T262 import matrix rather than being solved as the supply--use residual.
  This preserves the measured import level and is closer to the reference paper's stated intent of
  reading trade off the input--output table; it is also what removes the residual degree of
  freedom and makes the reconciliation of \S\ref{sec:calib-balance} necessary. The reconciliation
  retains it.
\item[Opening inventories are zero.] Sector inventories open at $S_i(0)=0$, as in the reference
  specification. (An earlier artifact derived opening inventories from the positive part of the
  negated statistical discrepancy; a signed accounting residual is not an inventory stock, and
  once the balance clears there is no residual to promote.)
\item[Policy rate on a quarterly basis.] The legacy field named \code{euribor} holds the U.S.\
  policy rate, stored as $r=(\text{annual rate}+1)^{1/4}-1$. Comparison against the
  annualised-percentage-point effective federal funds rate (EFFR) therefore requires
  $100\,((1+r)^4-1)$. At 2024Q4 this gives 4.571\,\% against a panel value of 4.65\,\%.
\end{description}

The firewall governs every component of \S\ref{sec:model} equally. The reconciliation restores an

the trend-growth rate is a trailing average of measured series under a rule frozen in advance.

never from their forecast performance.

\subsection{Absence of behavioural look-ahead}

The exogenous arrays for government consumption, exports and imports extend up to twelve quarters
past each origin in the artifact. This is behaviourally empty: only the element at the origin index
is ever read, once, as a scalar, at construction, after which the series evolve by their own
estimated processes. The claim was tested by mutation. Three runs at 2024Q4 under an identical seed
--- untruncated; truncated to the origin; and untruncated with \emph{every post-origin entry
overwritten by $-10^{99}$} --- produced bit-identical real GDP, nominal GDP and policy-rate paths,
with the poisoned run finite throughout. Truncation is applied anyway as past-only hygiene, and
every estimation window terminates at the origin.

\subsection{Why each difference is hard, not merely different}

\paragraph{Valuation bases.} A supply--use system can be stated at \emph{basic prices} (what the
producer receives) or at \emph{purchasers' prices} (what the buyer pays, including trade and
transport margins and net product taxes). The model's coefficients are defined on the basic-price
convention; the BEA tables are published at purchasers' prices. Bridging requires netting product
taxes and reallocating margins, and every bridge leaves a residual somewhere. Eurostat hands the
Austrian calibration a table that is already on the required basis; the U.S.\ calibration has to
construct one.

\paragraph{The industry/commodity axis.} Eurostat publishes symmetric product-by-product tables:
rows and columns index the same 64 objects. BEA publishes a \emph{make} table (which industry
produces which commodity) and a \emph{use} table (which industry consumes which commodity) over
non-identical axes --- 71 industries against 68 commodities. Projecting one onto the other is a
modelling choice, not a lookup. Put these two paragraphs together and the reconciliation of
\S\ref{sec:calib-balance} stops looking optional: a published system balances only in the basis
and on the axes in which it was published, the bridge and the projection each disturb the balance,
and taking imports from the measured T262 matrix removes the residual degree of freedom that could
have absorbed the disturbance. For a simulator whose capacity envelope is anchored to those same
accounts, the residual is not a bookkeeping blemish; it is a standing order for some sectors to
produce beyond their ceiling.

\paragraph{One structural year for fifteen years of origins.} The U.S.\ artifact contains 37 annual
arrays of length one --- the input--output block, firm counts, employees, population, census
unemployed and inactive, fixed assets, dwellings, capital consumption, tax and benefit aggregates,
and sectoral wages --- frozen at a single reference year. Out of the box only nine origins are
constructible. Unlocking the historical grid uses a single-array rewrite: shallow-copy the
calibration dictionary and set the year index to the origin's year, which makes the annual index
resolve to 1, the only valid index for a length-one array. All quarterly series run from 1996Q4,
so opening \emph{levels} scale with the era (model opening real GDP is $5.483\times10^{6}$ at
2019Q4, $3.745\times10^{6}$ at 2010Q2, $3.231\times10^{6}$ at 2005Q2). The consequence is a clean
and declarable split, which the results tables respect:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Flow targets} --- real GDP growth, deflator inflation, nominal GDP growth, the policy
  rate --- are initialised from quarterly series with full history. The annual structure enters as
  a fixed technology and demand-composition matrix, not as a level, and \S\ref{sec:vintage} shows
  the results are insensitive to which reference year supplies it.
\item \textbf{Stock-initialised targets} --- unemployment above all --- open at the structural
  year's level at \emph{every} origin. The unemployment \emph{level} is therefore mis-initialised
  at historical origins by construction; \S\ref{sec:results-unemp} scores the target anyway and
  reports the loss honestly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scientific assumptions register}
\label{sec:assumptions}

Every assumption on which a reported number depends is listed here with its justification and its
status. \textbf{[S]} marks a structural choice --- one that restores an accounting identity or
corrects an estimator that is wrong on its own terms, using past-only information. \textbf{[T]}
would mark behavioural tuning against forecast performance; no entry carries it, and none was done.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=4pt]

\item \textbf{Revised-data protocol; current-vintage truth, symmetric across models.} The scoring
panel is a current-vintage snapshot of 101 quarters, 2000Q3--2025Q3, used for estimation and truth
by every column in the field. Real GDP growth for 2010Q2 is the value as revised through 2026, not
the value available in 2010. \emph{Status:} a known limitation, symmetric across every model, and
the same design the reference paper adopts for the bulk of its evaluation. \emph{Flags:}
\code{real_time = false}, \code{origin_admissible = false}, \code{promotion_eligible = false}.

\item \textbf{One annual structure at every historical origin.} The 37 frozen annual arrays are
future information at origins before the reference year. \emph{Status:} asymmetric and ours alone
--- the sharpest departure from the reference paper. Bounded for flow targets, and now measured:
re-running the exercise under 2017 and 2012 structures moves the headline weighted ratio by at
most 0.0007 (\S\ref{sec:vintage}). Remains binding for the opening unemployment \emph{level}
(A10).

\item \textbf{Agent scale $10^{-5}$.} 130 firms. \emph{Justification:} an agent-count sweep from
130 to 2\,708 firms leaves the central results invariant. \emph{Status:} adequate for the mean,
\emph{inadequate for dispersion}: 54 of 68 sectors hold exactly one firm at this scale.

\item \textbf{Normal capacity utilisation $\omega=0.85$ [S, inherited].} Hard-coded upstream. It
fixes opening headroom at $1/0.85 - 1 = 17.6\,\%$ for every firm by construction.
\emph{Status:} an inherited magic number. Sourcing it from the Federal Reserve G.17 capacity
utilisation series \citep{frb_g17} would be a structural improvement; sweeping it to improve RMSE
would be tuning and is prohibited.

\item \textbf{Opening inventories $S_i(0)=0$ [S].} As in the reference specification. A signed
accounting residual is not an inventory stock; once the balance clears
(\S\ref{sec:calib-balance}) there is no residual to promote.

\item \textbf{Production-account anchoring, $\lambda = 0.983460$ [S].} Stated fully in
\S\ref{sec:calib-balance}: the aggregate excess of uses over supply decomposes exactly into two
measurement facts of the source data, one control must move for the reconciliation to be
well-posed, and the calibration anchors on the production account. $\lambda$ is fixed by the
accounting identity alone. The alternative closure (hold every budget; let a uniform 1.01\,\%
excess-demand wedge survive) was built and measured; it overshoots
(\S\ref{sec:evidence-gates}).

\item \textbf{Random-walk-with-drift growth expectations [S].} The correctly specified estimator
for an integrated-with-drift series, on past data only (\S\ref{sec:expectations},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammae}, Table~\ref{tab:gammae}). Gated behind a default-off model property, so the
Austrian and Italian calibrations remain bit-identical; measured contribution reported in
\S\ref{sec:evidence-attrib}.

\item \textbf{Trend supply growth at the observed rate [S].} $g$ is a trailing 40-quarter mean of
measured productivity and labour-force growth, re-estimated at each origin under a rule frozen in
advance (\S\ref{sec:trendgrowth}, \S\ref{sec:calib-trend}); it advances $\alpha_s, \bar w_s,
\kappa_s, \delta_s$ jointly, leaving unit labour cost and the replacement share invariant and
injecting no demand flow. Book capital remains constant ($K'=K$); capacity growth is carried by
the efficiency coefficients, so $\text{net }I=\Delta K$ holds trivially. \emph{Status:} an
observed-trend closure of a structural absence. The demand-side alternative --- investment
responding to the capacity gap --- was built in two designs and rejected on matched-seed
stability diagnostics, never on forecast errors (\S\ref{sec:evidence-invest}).

\item \textbf{No behavioural fitting.} Verified numerically (\S\ref{sec:firewall}); the
forecast-error override layer is never read on the paths that produce these results. The DSGE
benchmarks are estimated by posterior mode on pre-origin data under published priors
(\S\ref{sec:design-dsge}); their forecast performance never feeds back into their estimation.

\item \textbf{Unemployment is scored, with a declared initialisation defect.} The initial
unemployed stock derives from the frozen annual structure, so every historical origin opens at
that year's labour market rather than its own. The target enters the scored tables as its own
set; the \emph{level} errors this creates are reported as losses, and the coverage misses are
52--64\,\% above-band at every horizon --- the signature of a level defect, not a dynamics
defect (\S\ref{sec:results-unemp}). For the DSGE columns, which have no unemployment block,
cells come from a labelled per-origin Okun bridge (\S\ref{sec:design-dsge}); every table quoting
them carries the label.

\item \textbf{Seeds and Monte-Carlo policy.} Simulation seeds are
\[
  \mathrm{seed}(\text{origin},\text{path}) \;=\;
  \mathrm{hash}\big((\texttt{:abm\_revised\_v1},\,\text{origin},\,\text{path})\big) \bmod 2^{30},
\]
so no two cells share a stream. Every component experiment of \S\ref{sec:evidence} draws the
\emph{identical} seed stream, which makes each contrast matched-seed; the expectation branch
consumes exactly one Normal variate either way precisely to preserve that alignment. The DSGE
columns use fixed per-origin seed bases with a domain-separated per-path generator. Failed paths
are counted and left visible, never resampled; the count is zero everywhere.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Operators}

Forecast targets are constructed \emph{pathwise, before any ensemble operation}. Writing $rg$ and
$ng$ for a path's real and nominal GDP series and taking row~1 as the origin, so that horizon $h$
is row $h+1$:
\begin{align}
\text{real\_gdp} &= 400\,\big(\log rg_{h+1} - \log rg_{h}\big), \\
\text{gdp\_deflator} &= 400\,\big[(\log ng_{h+1}-\log rg_{h+1}) - (\log ng_{h}-\log rg_{h})\big], \\
\text{nominal\_gdp} &= 400\,\big(\log ng_{h+1} - \log ng_{h}\big), \\
\text{EFFR} &= 100\,\big((1+r_{h+1})^4-1\big).
\end{align}
The \emph{GDP deflator} is the ratio of nominal to real GDP --- the economy-wide price index
implied by the accounts --- so its log difference is nominal growth minus real growth. The
quarterly-to-annual-rate scaling cancels from log differences, so $400 = 4\times 100$ is the
complete annualisation. Unemployment has no field in the data structure and is read off agent
state inside the stepping loop, with $H^{\mathrm{act}}=1\,826$ --- the concept closest to the
labour-force denominator --- in the denominator.

\subsection{Ensemble functionals}

Two point forecasts are emitted per cell, as separate model columns: the \emph{mean} of the
pathwise-transformed values and the \emph{median}. This follows \citet{gneiting2011}: the optimal
point forecast depends on the loss function, the mean being the Bayes act under squared error and
the median under absolute error. Reporting both prevents a loss-function mismatch from being
mistaken for, or hidden inside, a model property. The full 500-path draw set is additionally
retained per cell, which is what makes the exact density scores of \S\ref{sec:design-scores}
possible.

\subsection{The comparator field: two DSGE columns}
\label{sec:design-dsge}

A DSGE model, for the reader who has not met one: a small set of optimising representative agents
--- a household, a firm sector, a central bank --- whose decision rules are derived from explicit
intertemporal optimisation under rational expectations, linearised around a balanced-growth
steady state. The result is a linear state-space system whose cross-equation restrictions tie
today's observables to expectations of tomorrow's, solved here by the \citet{sims2002}
\code{gensys} method and estimated by Bayesian methods: a prior over the structural parameters,
a Kalman-filter likelihood over the observables, and (in this work) the posterior \emph{mode} as
the parameter estimate at each origin. DSGE models are the institutional standard for structural
forecasting at central banks, which is what makes them the benchmark that matters
\citep{smetswouters2007}. Both columns are re-estimated from scratch at every one of the 61
origins on data through that origin only; per-origin estimation and solution status is a
published artifact; no origin is dropped.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline,itemsep=3pt]
\item[\code{dsge_small_nk}.] An \citet{anschorfheide2007}-type small New Keynesian model: three
  observables (real GDP growth, GDP-deflator inflation, the federal funds rate), eight states,
  three structural shocks --- the minimal rational-expectations system with a Phillips curve, an
  Euler equation and a Taylor rule. The solver mechanics are the repository's sealed
  implementation, validated bit-tight against the reference FRBNY \code{DSGE.jl} oracle; the
  column estimates all 13 parameters by posterior mode at every origin on exactly the panel
  columns the statistical family sees. All 61 estimations converged, the solution is determinate
  at every origin, and the Taylor principle holds at every mode ($\psi_1 \in [1.31, 1.54]$).
\item[\code{dsge_sw07}.] The \citet{smetswouters2007} medium-scale model --- the canonical
  empirical DSGE of the U.S.\ business cycle: sticky prices and wages, habit formation,
  investment adjustment costs, variable capacity utilisation, seven structural shocks, and a
  flexible-price shadow economy that defines the output gap the policy rule responds to. The
  implementation is a full 49-state transcription of the published replication, solved by a
  size-generic \code{gensys} that was oracle-verified to reproduce the repository's sealed
  validated solver exactly, and estimated by warm-started posterior mode at every origin under
  the published priors on the seven standard observables (output, consumption, investment and
  real-wage growth per capita, hours, deflator inflation, the federal funds rate) from 1966Q1
  through the origin. \textbf{The SW07 column sees more history and more series than any other
  column in the field} --- an information advantage granted to the challenger deliberately, and
  disclosed wherever its results are quoted. Its parameter estimates are canonical across all 61
  origins (trend growth 0.29--0.42\,\% per quarter per capita, Taylor coefficient 1.6--2.0,
  interest-rate smoothing $\approx0.82$); a continuation test at the first origin gained only
  $+2.45$ log-posterior from 8\,000 further optimisation steps, so the modes are materially
  converged.
\end{description}

Three conventions travel with every DSGE cell. Their predictive densities (500 paths, matching
the ABM) carry filtered-state and future-shock uncertainty at the posterior mode, \emph{without}
parameter uncertainty --- weaker than the reference paper's full Bayesian bands, stronger than
fixed parameters, and disclosed. Their policy-rate paths are floored at zero (the effective lower
bound, which neither model imposes internally). And their unemployment cells come from a
labelled per-origin \emph{Okun bridge} --- an auxiliary regression of the change in unemployment
on real growth, iterated over each model's own simulated growth paths --- because neither DSGE
has an unemployment block; the bridge is outside both cores and every table quoting DSGE
unemployment carries the label.

\subsection{Truth, scores, and inference}
\label{sec:design-scores}

Truth is the revised panel itself; the error sign convention is forecast minus actual. Target
transformations are \emph{not} uniform across the panel: the four GDP-family series are
$400\ln(\text{level}_t/\text{level}_{t-1})$; the unemployment rate is a quarterly mean of three
monthly levels with no transform; EFFR is the equal-weight mean of published business-day rates
within the quarter \citep{ny_effr}.

\emph{Point scores.} RMSE (root mean squared error) penalises large errors quadratically; MAE
(mean absolute error) linearly; MASE (mean absolute scaled error, \citealp{hyndmankoehler2006})
divides MAE by the in-sample mean absolute first difference of the same series, making it
scale-free. The \emph{weighted ratio} used for standings is a macro-average of matched cellwise
ratios,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{W} \;=\; \sum_{\text{targets}}\sum_{h}\;
  w_{\text{target}}\; w_h \;\frac{\text{score}_{\text{model}}(\text{target},h)}
  {\text{score}_{\text{anchor}}(\text{target},h)},
  \qquad
  w_h \in \{0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10\},
\end{equation}
with equal target weights and horizon weights declining in $h$. It is explicitly \emph{not} a
ratio of pooled losses, which would let one badly scaled target dominate. The scoring key is
(origin, target, horizon), and only cells where \emph{every} column in the field is present
survive --- 1\,415 common cells --- so no ranking can be a sample artifact. Three target sets are
scored: the headline pair \{real GDP growth, GDP-deflator inflation\}, the secondary pair
\{nominal GDP growth, EFFR\}, and unemployment as its own set.

\emph{Density scores.} The continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) generalises absolute error
to a full predictive distribution: it is the integrated squared difference between the forecast's
cumulative distribution and the step function at the realised value, so it rewards densities that
are both \emph{sharp} (narrow) and \emph{correct} (centred), and it reduces to absolute error for
a point forecast. It is computed exactly from the retained 500-path draws by the standard
pairwise-distance identity. \emph{Interval coverage} asks how often the realised value falls
inside a stated predictive interval: a well-calibrated 90\,\% interval should contain the truth
90\,\% of the time --- more often means the bands are wastefully wide, less often means
overconfidence.

\emph{Inference.} Rankings on 50--61 origins need error bars. Three standard instruments are
attached, all on matched cells. The \citet{dieboldmariano1995} test asks whether two models'
per-origin loss differences average to zero, with the \citet{harveyleybournenewbold1997}
small-sample correction and autocorrelation-robust variance (overlapping multi-quarter forecasts
are serially correlated by construction). The \citet{romanowolf2005} stepdown controls the
familywise error rate when one model is compared against a whole family at once --- it answers
``is the ABM better than \emph{each} challenger'' without the cherry-picking risk of many
unadjusted pairwise tests --- via a studentised stationary bootstrap on per-origin weighted
losses. The \citet{hansenlundenason2011} model confidence set inverts the question: it trims the
field to the subset of models that cannot be statistically distinguished from the best at a given
confidence level, so it reports how much the sample can actually separate.

\subsection{The opening-row measurement basis}

One artifact of initialisation must be declared because it affects the $h=1$ cell, which carries
the largest horizon weight. The initialiser writes row~1 on a different measurement basis from
rows~2 onwards: prices are normalised to 1 at initialisation, so real and nominal GDP coincide in
row~1 and the model-implied opening deflator is exactly 1.0. The opening row is the
\emph{model-implied} opening, not an observed control --- reassuring for leakage, unhelpful for
$h=1$ comparability. Dropping $h=1$ was rejected outright: it would render the grid incomplete and
the model unranked. It is reported and flagged.

\subsection{The preregistered bar, and the verdict stated first}
\label{sec:results-verdict}

The frozen protocol committed to a success bar before any scored table existed: the ABM is
superior if its weighted headline-pair RMSE ratio beats the best non-ABM column, \emph{including
both DSGE columns}, in at least two of the three tracks with Diebold--Mariano significance
reported per horizon, \emph{and} its density scores (CRPS, 90\,\% coverage) are no worse than the
best challenger's; if it instead wins only part of that, the part it wins is the claim published;
and a negative result is published with the same tables.

The verdict against that bar. The ABM takes the \textbf{best weighted headline-pair RMSE ratio of
all twenty columns in all three tracks} --- 0.828 / 0.828 / 0.792 (mean column; the median column
is marginally better at 0.827 / 0.827 / 0.791) --- against 0.857 / 0.856 for the best non-ABM
column in the two full-sample tracks (SW07) and 0.848 in the pandemic-masked track (the AR(1)).
The point condition is met in three of three tracks, not merely the required two. Romano--Wolf
stepdown rejects equal accuracy against \emph{both} DSGE columns at the 5\,\% level in every track
(adjusted $p = 0.0002$--$0.016$); against the statistical family the weighted-loss test is
marginal (adjusted $p \approx 0.08$). The ABM's CRPS beats the best challenger in all three
tracks. But its \textbf{90\,\% interval coverage fails the ``no worse than the best challenger''
clause in the two full-sample tracks} --- pooled headline coverage 0.832 / 0.812 / 0.906, against
a challenger within 0.005--0.008 of nominal --- and the miss is located: real-GDP coverage is
0.86--0.95 at every horizon, while the GDP-deflator bands are too narrow (0.70--0.79).
\textbf{The full bar is therefore not met}, and per its honest-success clause the claim this paper
publishes is the conditional one:

\begin{quote}
On revised data, at the reference paper's own evidentiary standard, the ABM forecasts the headline
pair \{real GDP growth, GDP-deflator inflation\} \emph{more accurately in point terms than every
statistical and equilibrium challenger in the field, in all three tracks} --- the margin over both
DSGE columns is significant at 5\,\% under multiplicity control, and the real-GDP margin over SW07
is significant at business-cycle horizons --- \emph{and more sharply in CRPS terms}, while its
GDP-deflator intervals are under-dispersed relative to the best-calibrated challenger. The full
preregistered bar fails only on that interval-calibration leg.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Unemployment: scored, and lost on the level}
\label{sec:results-unemp}

Unemployment enters the scored tables as its own target set, and the ABM does not win it: at
VAR(1) parity on the weighted ratio unmasked (0.995 mean / 0.974 median, 12th--13th of 20)
against persistence models at 0.65, and far behind in the masked track (5.3--5.4, where masking
removes exactly the cells in which the VAR anchor itself exploded, deflating every level-biased
column's denominator). The reason is measured and initialisation-shaped, not dynamic: every
origin opens at the frozen structural year's labour market rather than its own, the bias column
of Table~\ref{tab:percell} is a near-constant $-1.9$ to $-2.4$\pp, and the interval misses are
52--64\,\% \emph{above}-band at every horizon --- realised unemployment sitting above bands that
opened too low. The model's unemployment \emph{dynamics}, by contrast, now behave: ensemble
dispersion rises monotonically with horizon to 1.42\pp\ at $h=12$, which is $0.83\times$ the
realised cross-origin dispersion (Figure~\ref{fig:f7}) --- and \S\ref{sec:evidence-gates} shows
by ablation that without the trend-growth component the same statistic collapses to $0.27\times$
as every path exhausts the labour pool. Scoring the level honestly requires origin-correct
opening labour stocks --- a data acquisition, not a model change, and it is named in
\S\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Which components carry the accuracy: matched-seed evidence}
\label{sec:evidence}

A model that wins a horse race invites one question above all: \emph{which part of it is doing
the work?} Because every component of \S\ref{sec:model} is flag-gated, each can be switched off
and the entire 61-origin exercise re-run under the identical seed stream --- every path draws the
same random numbers, so differences are the component's causal effect, not Monte-Carlo noise ---
and scored on the identical common cells. Two rejected designs are reported alongside, because a
design space is only credible if it contains something that failed.

\subsection{What the unreconciled accounts do to the economy}
\label{sec:evidence-recon}

The unreconciled ablation is not merely worse; it is worse in a way that identifies the
mechanism, which is why the reconciliation can be called a component rather than a patch.
Unreconciled, the model under-forecasts real GDP growth by $-1.3$ to $-2.9$\pp\ at every horizon
(and $-7.3$\pp\ at $h=2$; Figure~\ref{fig:f3}). Matched-seed decomposition attributes 76\,\% of
the average shortfall to the opening-balance defect colliding with the frozen capacity envelope
--- 12.9--14.4\,\% of gross output produced under a binding ceiling every quarter, exports
filling only 87\,\% of demand, over-demanded sectors pinned at their caps while under-demanded
capacity idles uselessly --- and 24\,\% to the expectation shrinkage of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammae}. The diagnosis made a falsifiable prediction: a \emph{quantity-rationing}
defect should leave the \emph{price} block untouched when repaired. It held --- the GDP-deflator
column is bit-identical at $h=1$ between the two runs and its ratios differ in the third decimal
elsewhere, while real-GDP bias falls by two orders of magnitude, to $|{\cdot}|\le0.25$\pp\
(Table~\ref{tab:percell}). A dose--response check completes the identification: relieving the
capacity envelope by $+30\,\%$ in the unreconciled economy recovers most of the lost growth,
while the same relief added \emph{on top of} the reconciliation moves nothing --- the two
interventions are substitutes for one binding constraint, which is what the mechanism claims.

\subsection{What trend supply growth buys: the two gates}
\label{sec:evidence-gates}

Two simulation-internal gates certify the trend-growth component; neither consulted a forecast
error.

\emph{Utilisation and capacity.} Matched-seed 24-quarter simulations at three origins --- twice
the scored window, because the question is asymptotic behaviour: without the component,
utilisation drifts from its 0.850 opening to 0.98--0.99 by quarter 24 with zero capacity growth;
the economy grows only by draining its fixed 17.6\,\% headroom, and beyond roughly eight to ten
years it must stall. With the component, utilisation stabilises at 0.87--0.91 while capacity
grows 10--13\,\% over the six years --- the calibrated trend. Growth is no longer headroom
drainage, and the validity restriction that would otherwise cap the model at $h\le12$ on the
supply side is lifted. (The rejected alternative closure of assumption A6 --- keep every measured
budget and let a uniform 1\,\% excess-demand wedge survive --- fails this same gate from the
other side: it exhausts its headroom in about four years, which is why its smaller measured
headline bias was never taken as evidence in its favour.)

\emph{Unemployment dynamics.} Without the component, simulated demand growth must be met by
employment growth out of the fixed pool: unemployment falls mechanically at every origin, path
dispersion collapses to $0.27\times$ the realised cross-origin dispersion by $h=12$ as every path
reaches the floor, and end-of-horizon unemployment sits at 0.8--1.1\,\% --- roughly a third of
the lowest rate the United States has recorded in sixty years. With the component, dispersion
rises monotonically to $0.83\times$ realised at $h=12$ and the rate holds a stable, widening
band (Figure~\ref{fig:f7}). The remaining coverage misses are 52--64\,\% above-band at every
horizon: the frozen opening \emph{level} of A10, not the dynamics.

\subsection{The investment designs that were rejected}
\label{sec:evidence-invest}

The natural objection to \S\ref{sec:trendgrowth} is that capacity growth should come from
\emph{investment} --- firms observing demand pressure and accumulating capital --- rather than
from an exogenous efficiency trend. Two such designs were built, run matched-seed, and rejected
on stability diagnostics alone, before any forecast was scored; both remain runnable in the
repository. A \emph{capacity-gap accelerator} ($I^d$ closing a fraction $\nu=0.0625$ of the gap
between demand-implied and actual capital each quarter) and a \emph{balanced-growth
net-investment flow} ($g\,K_i$ added to desired investment) each destabilise the economy the
same way: the injected investment demand lands in a rationed goods market, raises realised
sales, which raises adaptive sales expectations, which raises next quarter's demand --- a
multiplier--accelerator spiral that the model's decentralised markets amplify rather than damp.
Measured on matched seeds: real growth 5.5--8.2\,\% annualised and unemployment to zero within
4--8 quarters, under either design. That fragility is itself a finding about this model class at
this calibration: the demand side cannot absorb a net-investment flow without a boom, so trend
capacity growth has to enter on the supply side --- which is also where, in the data, most of it
lives (measured productivity growth). A demand-side investment block stable enough to carry
trend growth is an open design problem, not a small parameter choice.

\section{Limitations and open problems}
\label{sec:limits}

Ranked by inferential risk to the results above.

\subsection{Revised data, mixed vintage}
The entire exercise estimates and scores on a current-vintage panel, and the annual structure is
carried backwards to historical origins. The first limitation is symmetric across all twenty
columns and matches the reference paper's own design; the second is asymmetric and ours alone,
now bounded for the input--output block by the direct measurement of \S\ref{sec:vintage} (moving
the structure to 2017 or 2012 changes the headline ratio by at most 0.0007) but still binding for
counts, taxes and the opening labour stock. No real-time information set was reconstructed, so
nothing here is a claim about what a forecaster would have produced in 2010; the route to that
claim is a data-acquisition programme --- year-by-year annual history for the 37 structural
arrays, plus a real-time truth layer of the \citet{croushorestark2001} kind --- not a modelling
one.

\subsection{The unemployment opening level}
Unemployment is scored and lost (\S\ref{sec:results-unemp}), for a measured reason: every origin
opens at the structural year's labour market. Fetching historical annual Current Population
Survey stocks would make the level scoreable at historical origins; the dynamics, repaired by the
trend-growth component, are already there. Separately, the labour market lives on a fixed agent
pool: with supply growth registered in productivity rather than population, unemployment remains
a valid object only within the forecast window --- on 24-quarter simulations the pool still
exhausts beyond roughly four years. Population growth in the agent count itself is the clean
long-run fix and is an intrusive change to the initialisation machinery.

\subsection{Interval calibration of the deflator and the policy rate}
The one leg of the preregistered bar the model fails. The deflator ensemble is under-dispersed at
every level (90\,\% coverage 0.70--0.79); the policy-rate bands collapse with horizon (0.16 at
$h=12$). Both are mechanism-level facts --- the price block inherits too little of the quantity
dispersion, and the Taylor-rule path is too deterministic --- and both are named, measured
targets for the next model change. Widening by post-processing would be a publication-layer
correction, not a model improvement, and is prohibited by the firewall from touching these
tables.

\subsection{DSGE estimation is posterior-mode, not full Bayes}
The reference paper's DSGE comparison used full Markov-chain Monte Carlo (250\,000 draws per
origin); ours estimates the mode of the same posterior at every origin and simulates densities
at that mode. Stronger than fixed parameters, weaker than full MCMC --- parameter uncertainty is
absent from the DSGE bands, which flatters their sharpness and is disclosed wherever they are
quoted. Full-chain estimation at every origin is a compute programme, not a conceptual gap.

\subsection{Scale, and partial density evaluation}
All headline results are at scale $10^{-5}$ (130 firms). An agent-count sweep shows the central
results scale-invariant, but 54 of 68 sectors hold exactly one firm, which makes cross-sectional
dispersion and tail behaviour unreliable; a production run at $\ge10^{-4}$ costs roughly
$8\times$ the runtime and should precede any density claim finer than the CRPS and coverage
reported here. No probability-integral-transform histogram or log score was computed.

\subsection{Absent bridges}
Three of the eight registered panel targets are unserved: the personal consumption expenditures
price index and its core variant need a consumption-basket price bridge the 68-commodity model
does not expose, and payroll employment targets an establishment count on a different universe
from the model's firm employment. The model is compared on five of eight targets, and the
repository's semi-structural comparator (whose native set includes the PCE index) cannot be
matched cell-for-cell in this field.

\subsection{Separation from the statistical family is marginal}
Romano--Wolf separates the ABM from both DSGE columns at 5\,\% in every track, but from the best
statistical columns only at $p \approx 0.08$ on weighted losses --- pandemic-era origins dominate
the loss metric and the historical-mean constant is loss-close there. Margins of a few percent
between adjacent statistical columns should not be treated as established; the model confidence
set makes the sample's limited resolving power explicit.

\subsection{Reproducibility notes}
The pipeline's legacy baseline builder cannot regenerate its own pre-reconciliation baselines;
the checked-in artifacts load and run correctly, and the structural-vintage builder used in
\S\ref{sec:vintage} was golden-tested against the shipped 2024 artifact (bit-exact), so the
provenance chain for every scored input is intact. The ensemble caches regenerate byte-for-byte
under the current source tree, which was verified for all 3\,660 rows of each frozen ablation
column before scoring.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An agent-based model of the United States, calibrated from public data under a verified
no-fitting rule and completed with three structural components --- exactly balanced opening
accounts, drift-form growth expectations, and observed trend growth in supply --- was evaluated
under the reference protocol of \citet{poledna2023} against a twenty-column field that includes
ten statistical benchmarks and two recursively re-estimated DSGE models, with formal inference,
exact density scores, matched-seed component attribution, and a structural-vintage robustness
sweep. Three findings are worth carrying away.

\emph{First, in a structural simulator the accounting is load-bearing, and it is where most of
the forecasting accuracy lives.} The largest single contributor to the ABM's standing is not a
behavioural rule but an identity: making the opening supply--use system balance exactly, in the
model's own valuation basis, on its own axes. A published table balances only as published;
after the valuation bridge and the industry-to-commodity projection a residual survives, and a
model whose capacity envelope is anchored to those accounts converts the residual into a
permanent, sector-locked rationing regime ($-2.75$\pp\ of growth bias, in this calibration).
Reduced-form models cannot exhibit this failure class --- they have no identities to violate ---
which cuts both ways: the ABM is exposed to it, and repairing it is a real, attributable source
of forecasting skill. The repair also made a falsifiable prediction --- a quantity defect should
leave the price block untouched --- that held to the bit.

\emph{Second, a fixed-population, fixed-productivity economy cannot host trend growth, and the
stable way to give it one is on the supply side.} Without trend supply growth the model must
convert demand trend into employment trend until the labour pool is exhausted, and output trend
into utilisation until capacity binds --- both measured, both terminal. Registering observed
productivity-plus-labour-force growth in the supply coefficients repairs the labour market's
dynamics and lifts the capacity ceiling while leaving headline accuracy essentially unchanged
--- evidence that the model's forecasting skill was never the artifact of a supply pathology.
The demand-side alternative, investment responding to the capacity gap, destabilises the economy
in two independent designs: a rationed goods market with adaptive expectations amplifies any
injected investment demand into a boom. That fragility is a genuine property of the model class
at this calibration, and it marks stable endogenous investment-driven growth as the design
problem standing between this model and multi-year use.

\emph{Third, against the strongest available challengers the ABM's advantage is real, specific,
and honestly bounded.} The margin over a fully re-estimated Smets--Wouters system --- itself
granted more data than any other column, and better than every statistical model --- is
significant under multiplicity control in every track, and it is concentrated where an
agent-based model ought to earn it: real activity at business-cycle horizons, with inflation
accuracy statistically indistinguishable and the density advantage carried by sharpness. Against
simple statistical models the separation is marginal on this sample. The deflator's intervals
are too narrow, and that single calibration leg fails the preregistered superiority bar, so the
claim published is the conditional one of \S\ref{sec:results-verdict} --- which is also,
precisely, what the evidence supports. What stands between this diagnostic and a real-time
forecasting claim is not further tuning --- the firewall forbids it --- but data: vintage-correct
structural inputs, origin-correct opening labour stocks, and full-posterior DSGE baselines. Each
is named, none is done, and the apparatus to score them already exists.

benchmark path: the runner raises an error unless the Julia and BLAS thread counts are both 1.

\hspace*{2em}output/us\_forecasting/stage2b\_full/v3\_headline 500 headline\_v3\\

\hspace*{2em}\ldots{} v3\_ar1 500 headline\_v3\_ar1\\

\hspace*{2em}--project=scripts/us scripts/us/forecasting/benchmarks/dsge\_columns/\textbackslash\\

\hspace*{2em}output/us\_forecasting/stage2b\_dsge\_columns both\\[4pt]

\hspace*{2em}\ldots{}/stage2b/run\_stage2b\_scorecard.jl \textbackslash\\

\hspace*{2em}\ldots{}/stage2b/run\_stage2b\_inference.jl output/us\_forecasting/stage2b\_scorecard\\
\hspace*{2em}\ldots{}/stage2b/verdict\_stage2b.jl output/us\_forecasting/stage2b\_scorecard\\

\hspace*{2em}--calibration=data/us/calibration/US\_2017\_calibration\_object\_reconciled.jld2)\\[4pt]

python3 paper/figures\_src/make\_figures.py\\
cd paper \&\& latexmk -pdf US\_ABM\_Forecasting\_Paper.tex

Step~2 resumes from its own ensemble cache, so an interrupted run continues rather than
restarting; the composite scorecard of step~4 is a re-score off the caches (seconds), and it
asserts full origin coverage for every column before scoring. Every figure is regenerated by
step~7 directly from committed CSVs; no figure contains a hand-entered number, and the generator
pins \code{SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH} so a re-run reproduces each figure byte for byte.

\subsection{Identity seals}

Every run manifest seals the identities that could move a number: the calibration artifact path
and SHA-256 (main artifact
\pathc{data/us/calibration/US_2024_calibration_object_reconciled.jld2}, digest
\code{57e23f4a...e514760}), the reconciliation mode and $\lambda$
(\code{final_demand_scaled}, \code{0.9834601934561227}), the expectation specification
(\code{rw_drift}), the panel SHA-256 (\code{f7bb26a4...bff851bbe}), the sealed numerical kernel
and source-tree digests, path counts (500 / burn-in 0 / 12 simulated quarters), and the Julia
version and thread counts (1.10.3 / 1 / 1). The trend-growth fixture and the DSGE column
provenance files carry their own SHA-256 sums and retrieval timestamps. The $\lambda$ semantics
are recorded verbatim in each manifest: \emph{``explicit accounting choice: the four final-demand
aggregates C, G, I and X (and capital\_\allowbreak consumption and gross\_\allowbreak
capitalformation\_\allowbreak dwellings, which set the investment budget) are scaled by lambda so
the artifact's expenditure aggregates match its production account and the opening commodity
balance clears exactly. lambda is fixed by the accounting identity alone and was not chosen with
reference to any forecast error.''}

\subsection{Seed policy}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item Simulation seeds are $\mathrm{hash}((\texttt{:abm\_revised\_v1},\,\text{origin},\,
      \text{path}))\bmod 2^{30}$: domain-separated by origin and path, so no two cells share a
      stream.
\item Every ablation and robustness variant draws the \emph{identical} seed stream as the model,
      which makes each contrast matched-seed; the expectation branch consumes exactly one Normal
      variate either way to preserve the alignment.
\item The seed is set \emph{immediately before} model construction, because the initial
      microstate is drawn from the global generator; seeding after construction yields a random
      initial state and is a silent error. A fresh model is constructed per path.
\item The DSGE columns use fixed per-origin seed bases ($7\,000\,000 + \text{origin index}$ and
      $8\,000\,000 + \text{origin index}$) with an SHA-domain-separated per-path generator, so
      their draws are version-independent and reproducible.
\item Failed paths are counted and left visible, never resampled. The count is 0 in every run.
\end{itemize}
